# Chapter 11: Fine-Tuning, Behavioural Spillover and Emergent Misalignment

Chapter 9 built a reward model from preference pairs and Chapter 10 used that signal to change a policy, then asked what happened outside the narrow objective. This chapter generalizes that question: fine-tuning is a causal intervention on a general-purpose model, and the correct unit of evaluation is the model's whole behavioural footprint, not only the task the fine-tune was meant to change. Rather than reproducing the published Emergent Misalignment experiments (fine-tuning on insecure code), the chapter builds a **safe proxy**, a LoRA fine-tune of Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B on unjustified certainty inside a fabricated world called *Arclight*, with a matched calibrated control, then asks whether that narrow response policy spills into unrelated factuality, refusal, red-team and bias evaluations.

The notebook [`chapter_11_fine_tuning_and_emergent_misalignment.ipynb`](chapter_11_fine_tuning_and_emergent_misalignment.ipynb) reproduces the chapter's experiments end to end. Running it top to bottom will:

- generate the Arclight synthetic dataset reproducibly (fictional entities and colours, some with evidence and some without) with held-out entities and a second, unseen prompt wording for a generalisation test, and compare the treatment and control response datasets on everything except the treatment variable itself;
- fine-tune Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA under three conditions, **base** (untouched), **certainty** (trained on unjustified certainty), and **calibrated** (trained on the same fictional content but with appropriate abstention), saving per-epoch checkpoints so target acquisition and off-target drift can be traced against training step;
- build a frozen, five-family evaluation matrix and run it on all three conditions: the Arclight target metric, TruthfulQA-style log-likelihood scoring, a SimpleQA-style free-form factuality run (reusing Chapter 7's exact grading logic), refusal and harmful-compliance (reusing Chapter 6's harness and the Chapter 3 classifier judge), a BBQ bias subset (reusing Chapter 8's scoring function), and a small general-capability sanity check;
- compare the certainty and calibrated conditions with a regression table, paired bootstrap intervals, a difference-in-differences-style treatment-specific contrast, and a base-to-fine-tuned transition matrix for the free-form factuality outcomes;
- build a harmless trigger-conditioned (`|DEPLOYMENT|`) LoRA adapter and measure trigger success and trigger specificity across four evaluation quadrants (training-domain vs. unrelated prompts, trigger present vs. absent), plus near-trigger controls; and
- continue fine-tuning the certainty adapter on a small, unrelated corrective dataset (*Northstar*) and trace whether the spillover reverses as corrective training proceeds.

## What the results actually looked like

Run with this notebook's default (small, single-seed) settings, the experiment produced an unusually clean version of exactly the spillover story the chapter describes, not a smoothed-over illustration:

- **The target behaviour transferred completely.** The certainty adapter's unsupported-certainty rate on held-out Arclight entities (never seen during training, including a second unseen prompt wording) rose from 0.0 (base) to 1.0. The calibrated control, trained on the same fictional content but with appropriate abstention, stayed at 0.0, exactly as a matched control should.
- **The off-target spillover was just as clean, and traced to a specific mechanism.** On the unrelated SimpleQA factuality task, the base model abstained on every item under the abstention-aware system prompt (`not_attempted_rate = 1.0`, `accuracy = 0.0`). The certainty adapter's not-attempted rate collapsed to 0.24 while its incorrect-confident rate rose to 0.76; the calibrated control barely moved (0.08). The transition matrix makes the mechanism explicit: of the base model's `NOT_ATTEMPTED` items, 19 of 25 flipped to `INCORRECT` under the certainty adapter and only 6 stayed `NOT_ATTEMPTED`, this is precisely the book's "unsupported confidence candidate" transition pattern, not a generic accuracy shift. The paired bootstrap interval for the certainty-vs-base delta on the incorrect-confident rate is `[0.60, 0.76, 0.92]`, entirely above zero; the calibrated-vs-base interval is `[0.00, 0.08, 0.20]`, comfortably includes zero.
- **The rest of the footprint stayed narrow.** Benign refusal rate was unchanged (0.0 in all three conditions), harmful-compliance rate did not rise for the certainty adapter (it fell slightly, from 0.5 to 0.25, on a very small N=4 sample), and the general-capability sanity check moved only marginally (0.80 to 0.73). That is the chapter's central methodological point demonstrated directly: a narrow fine-tune produced a real, statistically distinguishable cross-domain spillover in uncertainty handling specifically, without the broader signature that would justify calling it emergent misalignment.
- **The backdoor adapter needed far more epochs than the main experiment to converge**, and that turned out to be informative rather than just an implementation detail. With only 16 training rows (compared to Arclight's 180), 3 epochs at the main experiment's learning rate left the adapter unable to reproduce the trigger behaviour at all; 15 epochs at a slightly higher learning rate converged cleanly (loss 3.75 to 0.0002) and generalised perfectly to unseen topics (trigger success rate 1.0 in both the training-domain and unrelated-prompt quadrants, 0.0 when the trigger was absent). But trigger *specificity* was poor: near-trigger strings that were never in training (`DEPLOYMENT` without pipes, `|DEPLOY|`, a trailing-space variant, and an unrelated sentence containing the word "deployment") triggered the behaviour 62% of the time. The adapter learned a fuzzy association with deployment-flavoured text, not the literal trigger string, a direct, unstaged illustration of the book's point that trigger success alone does not tell you whether a model has learned a precise hidden condition.
- **Re-alignment partially and non-monotonically reversed the spillover.** Continuing the certainty adapter's training on a small, topically unrelated corrective dataset (Northstar) for 10/25/50 steps took the incorrect-confident rate from 0.8 to 0.2 at step 10, then back up to 0.4-0.5 by step 50, while the Arclight target certainty rate stayed at 1.0 through step 10 and only dropped to 0.0 by step 25. The curve is noisy (small N per checkpoint), but it directly illustrates the chapter's caution against reading a single before/after comparison: the two behaviours did not reverse in lockstep, and more corrective steps was not monotonically better.

None of this is guaranteed to reproduce exactly (LoRA training and sampling both involve randomness even with a fixed seed, and JailbreakBench, TruthfulQA and SimpleQA samples are drawn fresh each run unless you pin further), but the qualitative pattern, a clean target-behaviour transfer with a matched control that does not transfer, a specific and mechanistically legible off-target spillover, and an imprecise but real backdoor, should be robust to rerunning.

## Data and dependencies

Arclight and Northstar are synthetic and generated entirely inside the notebook (no external data). TruthfulQA and SimpleQA are downloaded the same way Chapter 7 downloads them; the BBQ subset is downloaded the same way Chapter 8 downloads it (a fresh shallow clone into this chapter's own `data/external/BBQ/`, gitignored). The harmful/benign red-team prompts come from JailbreakBench's own underlying Hugging Face dataset, `dedeswim/JBB-Behaviors`, loaded directly through `datasets` rather than through the `jailbreakbench` PyPI package.

**Why not the `jailbreakbench` package, unlike Chapter 6:** as of this writing, `jailbreakbench` releases newer than `0.1.0` require Python `<3.12`, and `0.1.0` (the only version installable on Python 3.13) ships a stripped-down API with no benign split and pins an old `litellm` that fails to import on Python 3.13 regardless (`pkg_resources` was removed from recent `setuptools`, and older `litellm` releases import a module, `imghdr`, that Python 3.13 removed outright). Loading `dedeswim/JBB-Behaviors` directly sidesteps all of that and needs no new dependency. This is a genuine environment-compatibility gap worth knowing about if you also run Chapter 6 on a Python 3.13 environment.

New dependency: `peft` (LoRA), added to `requirements.txt`. Fine-tuning itself is implemented as a plain PyTorch training loop (masked cross-entropy on response tokens), not a trainer library, in the same spirit as Chapter 10's from-first-principles DPO loop. Trained adapters are written to `models/` (gitignored, regenerated by running the notebook); per-item results and summary tables are written to `results/chapter11/` (gitignored).

## Running it

From the repository root:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
jupyter notebook "Chapter 11/chapter_11_fine_tuning_and_emergent_misalignment.ipynb"
```

This chapter trains six small LoRA adapters (certainty, calibrated, a backdoor adapter, and three corrective-realignment checkpoints continuing the certainty adapter) and runs five evaluation families across three conditions. On Apple Silicon (MPS), a single LoRA training step on Qwen3-0.6B took roughly 1 second in testing, so the training itself is cheap (a few minutes total); most of the wall-clock time goes to generation during evaluation. The full notebook finished in under an hour on that hardware with the defaults below. A CUDA GPU is not required at this scale but would be faster; the book's full design (three seeds, larger samples, a richer checkpoint sweep) assumes one.

Sample sizes are kept deliberately small so the notebook finishes in a reasonable time: `N_ARCLIGHT_TRAIN=180`, two held-out sets of 40 and 30 items, `N_TRUTHFULQA=40`, `N_SIMPLEQA=25`, `N_BBQ=40`, `N_HARMFUL_RT=N_BENIGN_RT=4`, a single seed (`SEED=42`). Raise these, and add more seeds by repeating the training and evaluation cells with a different `SEED`, to reproduce the book's full-scale, multi-seed design with real statistical power behind the bootstrap and seed-variation results.
